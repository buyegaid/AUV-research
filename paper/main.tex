\begin{abstract}
Accurate ocean current estimation is essential for feedforward compensation in autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) trajectory tracking control. Model-based current observers face a fundamental challenge: under hydrodynamic model mismatch, estimation accuracy degrades, while purely kinematic observers remain robust but converge slowly. This paper proposes a CFD-Prior Current Observer (PC-RCO) that combines a CFD pre-calibrated hydrodynamic drag model with two key mechanisms: (1) \emph{continuous Gauss-Markov (GM) regularization}, which applies a weak exponential decay toward the prior mean at every time step, providing robust regularization that dominates during low-excitation phases; and (2) \emph{adaptive update-rate clamping}, which estimates the instantaneous DVL noise level online and dynamically adjusts the per-step update bound to balance convergence speed against physical plausibility. Systematic validation on the XHY AUV platform across 6 model mismatch levels (0--50\%), 4 baseline methods, and controlled noise conditions demonstrates: (1) PC-RCO degrades only 42\% under 30\% CFD drag perturbation versus 77\% for the extended Kalman filter (EKF), while maintaining 3$\times$ better accuracy than the kinematic observer at zero mismatch; (2) continuous GM regularization alone contributes 57--218\% improvement in estimation accuracy under mismatch and high-noise conditions; (3) adaptive clamping reduces the maximum per-step estimation jump by 34$\times$ under high DVL noise ($\sigma=0.10$ m/s) compared to an unclamped variant, preventing physically impossible transient estimates without sacrificing steady-state accuracy. The proposed method provides a robust, physically interpretable current estimation framework for AUVs operating under hydrodynamic model uncertainty.

\textbf{Keywords:} autonomous underwater vehicle; ocean current observer; CFD prior; Gauss-Markov regularization; adaptive clamping; model mismatch robustness
\end{abstract}
