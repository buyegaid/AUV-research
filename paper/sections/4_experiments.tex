\section{Numerical Validation}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}

\textbf{Simulation platform}: MATLAB-based XHY AUV 6-DOF dynamics simulator using the CFD RANS-calibrated drag model (Table~\ref{tab:cfd}) as nominal dynamics, with time step $\Delta t=0.01$\,s and simulation duration $T=100$\,s.

\textbf{Current scenarios}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{Circular trajectory} (continuous maneuvering): AUV tracks a circular reference path of radius 50\,m at $u_d=1$\,m/s, providing sustained yaw excitation.
    \item \textbf{Straight trajectory} (low excitation): AUV follows a straight reference path at $u_d=1$\,m/s, testing the excitation gate under weak maneuvering.
    \item \textbf{Current step} (abrupt change): At $t=50$\,s, current jumps from $V_c=0.15$\,m/s, $\beta_c=30^\circ$ to $V_c=0.45$\,m/s, $\beta_c=60^\circ$, testing observer response to sudden current changes.
\end{enumerate}

All scenarios use a first-order Gauss-Markov current process (correlation time $\tau_c=100$\,s, mean $V_c=0.3$\,m/s, $\beta_c=45^\circ$, standard deviation $\sigma_V=0.1$\,m/s).

\textbf{Model mismatch}: Simulated by randomly perturbing CFD drag coefficients in the plant model. Each DOF is perturbed independently at levels of 0\%, 10\%, 20\%, and 30\%. The observer always uses the nominal CFD model.

\textbf{Baseline methods}:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{KIN}: Kinematic current observer \cite{liang2018three}---dynamics-model-free baseline
    \item \textbf{LESO}: Standard linear extended state observer---lumped disturbance estimation
    \item \textbf{PIESO}: Physics-informed ESO with embedded Gauss-Markov current prior
    \item \textbf{EKF-tuned}: CFD-augmented extended Kalman filter (14 states: $\bm{\nu}+\bm{c}+$force bias), with process noise covariance Q adaptively tuned per mismatch level
    \item \textbf{UCCO}: Proposed EG-UCCO (yaw-only feedforward)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Evaluation metric}: Root-mean-square error of current estimate, $\text{RMSE}_{V_c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\hat{\bm{c}}_i-\bm{c}_i^{\text{true}}\|^2}$ (m/s).

\textbf{Key findings}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item UCCO achieves the best accuracy in all 12 scenario-mismatch combinations (entries in bold).

    \item In the straight-line low-excitation scenario, UCCO retains its accuracy advantage (0.17 vs.\ 0.28--0.29\,m/s), demonstrating that the excitation gate does not degrade performance when updates are sparse.
    \item In the current step scenario, UCCO and EKF-tuned perform comparably (0.20 vs.\ 0.20--0.24), both responding rapidly to abrupt current changes.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:degradation} shows the degradation trend of each method under increasing model mismatch for the circular trajectory.

UCCO's degradation (+4.1\%) is comparable to the tuned EKF (+4.5\%), but UCCO's absolute accuracy remains approximately 30\% better across all mismatch levels. KIN and LESO are immune to model mismatch by design (they do not use the CFD model), but their absolute accuracy is substantially lower. Notably, an \emph{untuned} EKF (nominal Q matrix) under identical conditions suffers a 694\% degradation, collapsing from RMSE$_{V_c}=0.062$ to 0.492\,m/s---illustrating the sensitivity of EKF-based methods to parameter tuning and the inherent robustness of UCCO's gradient-based update mechanism.

\subsection{Gate Ablation Study}

To validate the excitation gate's practical contribution, we compared three gate configurations under three sensor noise levels. Results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:gate}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Excitation gate ablation (RMSE$_{V_c}$, m/s, circular trajectory)}
\label{tab:gate}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Gate configuration & No noise & Low noise & High noise \\
\midrule
Gramian gate ($\mu=10^{-8}$) & \textbf{0.195} & \textbf{0.263} & \textbf{0.512} \\
No gate (always update)      & 0.202 & 0.278 & 0.668 \\
High threshold ($\mu=0.01$)  & 0.334 & 0.334 & 0.334 \\
\midrule
Gate benefit (vs.\ no gate)  & +3.6\% & +5.6\% & \textbf{+23.3\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key observations}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item The Gramian gate outperforms the no-gate variant under all noise levels, with the benefit increasing with noise: +3.6\% (no noise), +5.6\% (low noise), +23.3\% (high noise). This confirms the gate's protective role under adverse sensing conditions.
    \item The high-threshold gate ($\mu=0.01$) reduces the excitation rate to 0\%, completely blocking current estimation. RMSE$_{V_c}=0.334$ equals the current mean baseline, underscoring the importance of proper threshold selection.
    \item The Gramian gate achieves 100\% excitation rate on the circular trajectory, but regulates the effective update step size through Gramian condition-number-dependent regularization.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sensor Noise Robustness}

Under low sensor noise conditions (DVL velocity noise $\sigma_v=0.02$\,m/s, heading noise $\sigma_\psi=0.5^\circ$), we conducted Monte Carlo validation with 10 random seeds. Statistical results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Monte Carlo validation (10 seeds, Mean$\pm$Std, RMSE$_{V_c}$ m/s)}
\label{tab:montecarlo}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Scenario & Mismatch & KIN & EKF-tuned & UCCO \\
\midrule
\multirow{2}{*}{Circ.} & 0\%   & 0.272$\pm$0.062 & 0.276$\pm$0.028 & 0.279$\pm$0.056 \\
                       & 20\%  & 0.272$\pm$0.062 & 0.292$\pm$0.017 & \textbf{0.270$\pm$0.048} \\
\midrule
\multirow{2}{*}{Str.}  & 0\%   & 0.278$\pm$0.063 & 0.282$\pm$0.017 & 0.306$\pm$0.057 \\
                       & 20\%  & 0.280$\pm$0.063 & 0.290$\pm$0.015 & \textbf{0.276$\pm$0.061} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Under sensor noise with zero model mismatch, methods perform comparably (within 1--8\%). However, when model mismatch increases to 20\%, UCCO achieves the best estimation in both circular and straight scenarios (0.270 and 0.276\,m/s, respectively). More importantly, UCCO's performance change from 0\% to 20\% mismatch is $-3.5$\% (improvement) for circular and $-9.8$\% (improvement) for straight, while EKF-tuned degrades by $+5.8$\% and $+3.2$\%, respectively. These results strongly support UCCO's core claim: \textbf{robust current estimation under hydrodynamic model mismatch}.

We note candidly that UCCO's estimation variance (std$\approx$0.05--0.06) is somewhat higher than EKF-tuned (std$\approx$0.02--0.03), indicating greater per-run fluctuation in the gradient-based update mechanism. This is an inherent characteristic of gradient methods and can be mitigated through sliding-window smoothing or reduced adaptive gain.
