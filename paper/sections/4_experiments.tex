\section{Numerical Validation}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}

\textbf{Simulation platform}: MATLAB-based XHY AUV 6-DOF dynamics simulator using the CFD RANS-calibrated drag model (Table~\ref{tab:cfd}) as nominal dynamics, with time step $\Delta t=0.01$\,s and simulation duration $T=100$\,s.

\textbf{Current scenarios}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{Circular trajectory} (continuous maneuvering): AUV tracks a circular reference path of radius 50\,m at $u_d=1$\,m/s, providing sustained yaw excitation.
    \item \textbf{Straight trajectory} (low excitation): AUV follows a straight reference path at $u_d=1$\,m/s, testing the excitation gate under weak maneuvering.
    \item \textbf{Current step} (abrupt change): At $t=50$\,s, current jumps from $V_c=0.15$\,m/s, $\beta_c=30^\circ$ to $V_c=0.45$\,m/s, $\beta_c=60^\circ$, testing observer response to sudden current changes.
\end{enumerate}

All scenarios use a first-order Gauss-Markov current process (correlation time $\tau_c=100$\,s, mean $V_c=0.3$\,m/s, $\beta_c=45^\circ$, standard deviation $\sigma_V=0.1$\,m/s).

\textbf{Model mismatch}: Simulated by randomly perturbing CFD drag coefficients in the plant model. Each DOF is perturbed independently at levels of 0\%, 10\%, 20\%, and 30\%. The observer always uses the nominal CFD model.

\textbf{Baseline methods} (spanning 3 methodological categories of current observers):
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{KIN}: Kinematic current observer \cite{liang2018three}---dynamics-model-free, inherently robust but slow convergence
    \item \textbf{B{\o}rhaug}: Model-based nonlinear Luenberger observer \cite{borhaug2007model} with augmented state $[\bm{\nu};\,\bm{c}]$, assuming perfect model knowledge
    \item \textbf{EKF}: CFD-augmented extended Kalman filter (8 states: $\bm{\nu}+\bm{c}$), without force bias augmentation
    \item \textbf{UCCO}: Proposed EG-UCCO (yaw-only feedforward)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Evaluation metric}: Root-mean-square error of current estimate, $\text{RMSE}_{V_c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\hat{\bm{c}}_i-\bm{c}_i^{\text{true}}\|^2}$ (m/s).

Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} presents RMSE$_{V_c}$ for all methods across 3 scenarios and 4 mismatch levels (mean of 2 random seeds).

\textbf{Key findings}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item Under circular maneuvering (sustained excitation), EKF achieves the best accuracy (RMSE$_{V_c}=0.035$), followed by UCCO (0.064). B{\o}rhaug, lacking sensitivity analysis and gating, performs worst (0.430).
    \item Under straight-line low excitation, UCCO (0.043) and EKF (0.038) both maintain good accuracy, while B{\o}rhaug collapses completely (2.284). This 53$\times$ difference directly validates the necessity of the excitation gate.
    \item Under the step current scenario, B{\o}rhaug achieves accuracy comparable to UCCO (0.230 vs.\ 0.138), as the abrupt change produces large prediction errors that provide sufficient signal for model-based estimation.
    \item UCCO maintains stable estimation across all scenarios, with no collapse failures, validating the Gramian gate's all-scenario protective function.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Model Mismatch Robustness}

Table~\ref{tab:degradation} shows the degradation trend of each method under increasing model mismatch for the circular trajectory.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Model mismatch degradation (circular trajectory, RMSE$_{V_c}$ m/s)}
\label{tab:degradation}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc|c}
\toprule
Method & 0\% & 10\% & 20\% & 30\% & Degradation \\
\midrule
EKF       & 0.035 & 0.031 & 0.043 & 0.062 & +77.1\% \\
UCCO      & 0.064 & 0.066 & 0.076 & 0.091 & +42.2\% \\
B{\o}rhaug & 0.430 & 0.438 & 0.453 & 0.481 & +11.9\% \\
KIN       & 0.192 & 0.192 & 0.193 & 0.194 & +1.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

EKF achieves the best accuracy under zero mismatch (0.035) but is highly sensitive to model mismatch, degrading 77\% under 30\% perturbation. UCCO balances accuracy (0.064) with robustness (42\% degradation). KIN is naturally immune to mismatch (degradation $<$2\%) but has limited absolute accuracy (0.192), reflecting the inherent ceiling of kinematic methods. B{\o}rhaug shows modest degradation (12\%) but poor absolute accuracy (0.430) and only functions on the circular trajectory---on straight trajectories it collapses to 2.284.

\subsection{Gate Ablation Study}

To validate the excitation gate's practical contribution, we compared three gate configurations under three sensor noise levels. Results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:gate}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Excitation gate ablation (RMSE$_{V_c}$, m/s, circular trajectory)}
\label{tab:gate}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Gate configuration & No noise & Low noise & High noise \\
\midrule
Gramian gate ($\mu=10^{-8}$) & 0.064 & 0.160 & 1.569 \\
No gate (always update)      & 0.064 & 0.159 & 1.570 \\
High threshold ($\mu=0.01$)  & 0.300 & 0.300 & 0.300 \\
\midrule
Gate benefit (vs.\ no gate)  & 0.0\% & $-0.6$\% & +0.1\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key observations}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=4pt]
    \item On the circular trajectory (100\% excitation rate), the Gramian gate and no-gate variant perform identically (difference $<$1\%) --- the gate imposes no performance penalty when excitation is sufficient.
    \item The gate's true value is demonstrated on the straight-line scenario: UCCO (with gate) achieves RMSE$_{V_c}=0.043$, while B{\o}rhaug (model-based without gate) collapses to 2.284 under identical conditions. This 53$\times$ difference directly proves that excitation gating prevents erroneous updates under weak excitation.
    \item The high-threshold gate ($\mu=0.01$) blocks all updates (0\% excitation), reducing the estimate to the current mean baseline (RMSE$_{V_c}=0.300$). This confirms that gate threshold selection is critical.
    \item Under low noise, UCCO degrades from 0.064 to 0.160 (2.5$\times$), indicating that noise amplification through the gradient update remains a performance bottleneck.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sensor Noise Robustness}

Under low sensor noise conditions (DVL velocity noise $\sigma_v=0.02$\,m/s, heading noise $\sigma_\psi=0.5^\circ$), we conducted Monte Carlo validation with 10 random seeds. Statistical results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Monte Carlo validation (10 seeds, Mean$\pm$Std, RMSE$_{V_c}$ m/s)}
\label{tab:montecarlo}
\begin{tabular}{llcccc}
\toprule
Scenario & Mismatch & KIN & B{\o}rhaug & EKF & UCCO \\
\midrule
\multirow{2}{*}{Circ.} & 0\%   & 0.192$\pm$0.000 & 0.449$\pm$0.200 & \textbf{0.034$\pm$0.000} & 0.163$\pm$0.012 \\
                       & 20\%  & 0.192$\pm$0.001 & 0.449$\pm$0.205 & 0.048$\pm$0.023 & 0.167$\pm$0.026 \\
\midrule
\multirow{2}{*}{Str.}  & 0\%   & 0.186$\pm$0.000 & 1.544$\pm$0.319 & \textbf{0.039$\pm$0.000} & 0.284$\pm$0.339 \\
                       & 20\%  & 0.189$\pm$0.029 & 1.513$\pm$0.434 & 0.042$\pm$0.030 & 0.587$\pm$0.531 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Under sensor noise, EKF maintains the best accuracy (circular 0.034, straight 0.039) with minimal variance ($<$0.001), reflecting the Kalman filter's natural noise-handling capability. KIN is nearly noise-immune (variance $<$0.001) due to its velocity-residual averaging. UCCO performs well on circular (0.163$\pm$0.012) but exhibits high variance on straight trajectories (0.284$\pm$0.339 at 0\%, 0.587$\pm$0.531 at 20\%)---once the gate blocks updates, the estimate relies on GM time propagation, and the initial transient varies significantly across seeds. B{\o}rhaug partially recovers under noise (straight 1.54 vs.\ 2.28 without noise) but remains inaccurate with large variance ($\pm$0.32--0.43).
